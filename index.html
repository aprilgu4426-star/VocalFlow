Quick, practical notes on the sound patterns that make spoken English flow.

Connected speech · Linking

Join a consonant to a following vowel

When one word ends in a consonant and the next begins with a vowel, connect them smoothly without adding a pause.

turn‿it‿on“Turn it on” · keep the words connected
Connected speech · Reduction

Let small grammar words go light

Common function words are often shorter and weaker in a sentence. Keep the key meaning words clearer.

/tə/ · /ən/ · /əv/to · and · of · reduced forms vary by context
Stress · Rhythm

Give content words the main beats

English often alternates prominent meaning words with quicker, quieter grammar words. Avoid giving every syllable equal weight.

SEND the FILE by FOUR.Make the key words prominent; keep the rest flowing
Pitch · Intonation

Use pitch to signal your meaning

A falling contour often signals completion. A rising contour can invite confirmation or show that your thought continues.

That works for you ↗?Let the pitch rise gently on a genuine check-in
Sound clarity · Pronunciation

Finish the final consonants

Clear word endings help listeners distinguish key workplace terms. Release the final sound without adding an extra vowel.

report /rɪˈpɔːt/ · task /tɑːsk/Example IPA uses a broad British-English reference
